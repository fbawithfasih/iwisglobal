Itimad-ud-Daulah's Tomb — often called the "Baby Taj" — is the tomb of Mirza Ghiyath Beg, father of the Mughal empress Nur Jahan. "Itimad-ud-Daulah" was the title he was given: he served as Lord of the Treasury and later rose to wazir under Emperor Jahangir. Nur Jahan completed the tomb in 1628, about seven years after his death, a few years before work began on the Taj Mahal.

## Ticket price

Foreign tourists pay ₹310 on regular days, ₹300 on Fridays. Indian and SAARC/BIMSTEC nationals pay ₹30 (₹25 on Fridays). Children under 15 enter free, all nationalities.

## Hours

Open sunrise to sunset, every day — including Fridays, when the [Taj Mahal](/agra/taj-mahal) is closed. Sunrise in Agra ranges from about 5:20 AM in June to about 7:10 AM in January; see the [month-by-month sunrise table](/guides/best-time-to-visit-taj-mahal#sunrise-timing-by-season).

## What you'll see

The tomb stands at the centre of a walled garden laid out on the Mughal *char bagh* (four-part) plan, on a raised sandstone terrace about 149 feet (45 m) square. The ground floor's central hall holds the tombs of Itimad-ud-Daulah and his wife, surrounded by smaller chambers with the tombs of other family members. Above it, a pavilion with an oblong dome sits over the central hall, and at each corner stands a round tower about 40 feet (12 m) high, topped with a marble kiosk. The walls and cenotaphs on the ground floor carry Persian inscriptions — passages from the Quran and other texts on the walls, and the names and titles of those buried on the cenotaphs.

## Getting there

The tomb is on the left bank of the Yamuna, next to Chini-ka-Rauza — the same side of the river as [Mehtab Bagh](/agra/mehtab-bagh), across the water from central Agra, the Taj Mahal and [Agra Fort](/agra/agra-fort). See our [Agra travel hub](/agra) for the rest of what's nearby.
