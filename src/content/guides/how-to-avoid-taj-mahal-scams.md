**Most Taj Mahal scams work the same way: someone offers to solve a problem you don't actually have.** Your ticket is "invalid", the queue is "impossible", your hotel is "closed", the train is "cancelled". The fix is also the same every time: check the claim yourself, using an official source, before you hand over money. In September 2026 alone, Agra police arrested 50 people in a 15-day crackdown on tourist scams near the Taj Mahal ([Amar Ujala, 11 September 2026](https://www.amarujala.com/video/uttar-pradesh/agra/video-tourist-scammers-target-taj-mahal-visitors-50-arrested-in-15-day-crackdown-agra-up-2026-09-11)), so these aren't rare stories. Here are the ones that matter, in the order you'd meet them.

## Before you travel: fake ticket websites

In April 2026 the Archaeological Survey of India (ASI), which runs the Taj Mahal, warned tourists about a fake booking site, `Ticketstajmahal.com`. Tickets bought there are invalid and won't get you in ([ETV Bharat, 8 April 2026](https://www.etvbharat.com/en/state/taj-mahal-ticket-scam-asi-warns-tourists-against-fake-booking-websites-enn26040802243)).

ASI's official ways to buy tickets, as listed in that warning:

- `asi.paygov.org.in` and `asi.payumoney.com`
- The QR codes at the Taj Mahal's entry gates and ticket windows
- ASI's WhatsApp booking number, +91 84228 89057

ASI's own advice: check the website's spelling carefully, because fraudsters build lookalike sites; don't book through social media ads or unknown links; and never share your bank details or an OTP on an unofficial site. For what the tickets should cost, see our [Taj Mahal ticket price guide](/guides/taj-mahal-ticket-price-2026). A price far above ASI's is a warning sign.

## In Delhi: the "helpful stranger" scams

Most Delhi scams aimed at Taj Mahal visitors start with an unprompted offer of help. The UK government's travel advice for India says to "avoid people at railway stations offering tickets and tours" and to use app-based taxis or pre-paid taxi counters rather than hailing cabs on the street ([GOV.UK, India safety and security](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/india/safety-and-security)). The common versions, all described by travellers who've met them ([Laure Wanders, Delhi scams](https://www.laurewanders.com/scams-in-delhi/)):

| What you're told | What's really happening | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| "Your train is cancelled, come to this ticket office" | A fake counter outside the station sells an overpriced package instead | Check your train yourself on the official railway app or at the counter inside the station |
| "This is the government tourist office" | A private agency that pays drivers to bring tourists in | Don't book anything at an office a driver took you to |
| "Your hotel has closed" | A redirect to a hotel that pays commission | Call your hotel directly on the number from your booking |
| "I know a better place for tours and shopping" | A taxi or rickshaw driver on commission | Use Uber, Ola or a pre-paid taxi, and decline the detour |

## At the Taj Mahal: gate touts and "skip the line" offers

On 24 September 2026, Agra police arrested a guide after a video from an Irish couple went viral. Two guides at the Taj Mahal's western gate had told the couple that getting in without a long wait would be difficult without their help, then demanded about ₹1,000 for it. Police began proceedings to cancel both guides' licences ([Aaj Tak, 25 September 2026](https://www.aajtak.in/uttar-pradesh/story/irish-couple-scam-agra-taj-mahal-guide-arrested-illegal-money-demand-lclam-strc-2652927-2026-09-25)).

The pattern to watch for:

- **Anyone selling a way around the queue.** There's no paid side entrance. Pay only at the official ticket windows, QR codes or ASI's websites listed above.
- **"The Taj is closed today."** It's closed on Fridays only ([official Taj Mahal website](https://www.tajmahal.gov.in/)). On any other day, don't accept a detour because of it.
- **A "guide" who approaches you** rather than one you booked. If you want a guide on the spot, agree the full price and what it includes before you start, and ask to see their licence.
- **Shopping stops you didn't ask for.** Marble-inlay and gem shops that pay guides a commission are the classic end to a Taj Mahal tour. You can always say no and ask to go back.

## How to check any tour operator before you pay

These checks take a few minutes and work for any company, including ours:

1. **Look up their GST number.** Every registered Indian business has a 15-character GSTIN. Enter it in the GST portal's free [Search Taxpayer](https://services.gst.gov.in/services/searchtp) tool, and check that the legal name and state match what the company claims.
2. **Check the address is real.** A registered address on the website should match the GST record.
3. **See the full price in writing before you pay.** It should say whether monument tickets, the guide and the car are included.
4. **Pay through a proper payment gateway, not a personal bank or UPI account.** Card and gateway payments leave a record you can dispute; cash and transfers to individuals don't.
5. **Look for a complaints contact.** Online sellers in India must name a grievance officer under the Consumer Protection (E-Commerce) Rules, 2020.
6. **Read reviews from verified guests,** on more than one site, and look at the recent ones.
