## The Taj Mahal in one paragraph

Mumtaz Mahal died on 17 June 1631 in Burhanpur while giving birth to her fourteenth child. Shah Jahan began her tomb in Agra, on the bank of the Yamuna, in 1632. UNESCO's World Heritage listing records that the mausoleum was finished in 1648 and the mosque, guest house, main gateway and outer courtyard by 1653. Shah Jahan was deposed by his son Aurangzeb in 1658, held in the Agra Fort, and buried beside his wife in 1666. Mughal court histories called the building the *Rauza-i-Munavvara*, the "illumined tomb".

## Myth 1: "The Taj Mahal was a Hindu temple called Tejo Mahalaya"

**What the record says:** The idea that the Taj Mahal was originally a Shiva temple has circulated for decades and has been raised in court several times. In 2017 the Archaeological Survey of India (ASI), which manages the monument, told a court in Agra that the Taj Mahal is a tomb and that there is no evidence of a temple at the site ([Times of India](https://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/city/agra/for-the-first-time-asi-tells-court-taj-is-not-a-temple-but-a-tomb/articleshow/60224065.cms)). In May 2022 the Allahabad High Court dismissed a petition asking for a "fact-finding committee" on the monument's history, saying such questions belong to historians, not courts ([Indian Express](https://indianexpress.com/article/explained/explained-22-locked-rooms-at-the-taj-mahal-and-a-mystery-that-isnt-7916262/)).

**The short answer:** The documented history, the Mughal court records and the ASI all describe a 17th-century Mughal tomb.

## Myth 2: "The 22 locked rooms hide a secret"

**What the record says:** The "22 rooms" are a row of arched cells along a corridor in the lower levels of the riverside platform. They are maintained by the ASI and kept closed to visitors for safety and crowd management. When the Allahabad High Court dismissed the 2022 petition to open them, the ASI's account, reported by the Indian Express, was that nothing of significance is inside, and photographs of conservation work in the cells were published at the time.

**The short answer:** Locked does not mean secret. Many monuments keep service areas closed.

## Myth 3: "Shah Jahan cut off the craftsmen's hands"

**What the record says:** No contemporary source records it. The story, that the emperor maimed the builders so they could never build anything as beautiful again, is a folk tale told about several famous buildings around the world. The same families of inlay workers continued their craft in Agra, and you can still watch marble inlay being made in workshops in the city today.

**The short answer:** A legend with no evidence behind it.

## Myth 4: "Shah Jahan planned a Black Taj Mahal across the river"

**What the record says:** The idea of a matching tomb in black marble for Shah Jahan, opposite the Taj, comes from a 17th-century traveller's account and has never been backed by evidence. The site across the river is [Mehtab Bagh](/agra/mehtab-bagh), a Mughal moonlight garden aligned with the Taj Mahal. It is the best place to see the monument at sunset.

**The short answer:** Mehtab Bagh is real; the Black Taj is not.

## Myth 5: "20,000 workers built it in 22 years"

**What the record says:** This one is broadly true. Britannica puts the workforce at more than 20,000 and the building time at a little over two decades for the whole complex ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/question/How-was-the-Taj-Mahal-built)). Masons, inlayers, calligraphers and dome builders came from across the Mughal Empire, Central Asia and Iran.

**The short answer:** True, with the caveat that the tomb itself was finished in about 16 years and the full complex in about 21.

## Myth 6: "It cost 32 million rupees"

**What the record says:** That figure is the estimate usually quoted from the period. Converting a 17th-century cost into today's money is guesswork, so be wary of any precise dollar figure you see online.

**The short answer:** The 32 million rupee figure is a historical estimate; modern conversions are not reliable.

## Myth 7: "The minarets lean outward so they would fall away in an earthquake"

**What the record says:** The four minarets do stand very slightly off vertical, and the explanation most often given is that they would fall away from the tomb rather than onto it. It is a sensible idea, but no Mughal document states that this was the builders' intention.

**The short answer:** A popular explanation, not a documented one.

## Myth 8: "Mumtaz Mahal and Shah Jahan lie in the main hall"

**What the record says:** The two marble cenotaphs you see inside the main chamber are symbolic. The real graves are in the crypt directly below, a common arrangement in imperial Mughal tombs.

**The short answer:** You are looking at memorials, not graves.

## Myth 9: "The Taj Mahal is perfectly symmetrical"

**What the record says:** Almost. The whole complex is laid out in mirror image, with a red sandstone mosque on one side matched by a guest house on the other. The single exception is Shah Jahan's own cenotaph. It was placed beside his wife's after his death in 1666, and it is larger than hers, so it sits off the central axis.

**The short answer:** Symmetrical, apart from the emperor himself.

## Myth 10: "It was built only for love"

**What the record says:** Grief was the reason, but not the only message. The tomb stands at the end of a four-part *charbagh* garden meant to evoke the gardens of paradise, and its scale and white marble announced the wealth of the Mughal Empire at its height. It is a love story and a statement of power at the same time.

**The short answer:** Both love and empire.

## How to see the real story in Agra

The story makes most sense in this order:

1. **The Taj Mahal**, for the gateway, the garden, the calligraphy and the cenotaphs. Ask your guide to show you where the real graves lie.
2. **The Agra Fort**, for the Musamman Burj, the marble tower where Shah Jahan is said to have spent his last years looking at the Taj.
3. **Mehtab Bagh**, for the "Black Taj" site and the sunset view across the river.
4. **Itimad-ud-Daulah**, the "Baby Taj", the tomb of Mumtaz Mahal's grandfather, with the marble inlay that came before the Taj.

The Taj Mahal is **closed on Fridays**. It opens 30 minutes before sunrise and closes 30 minutes before sunset. ## Frequently asked questions

**What is the real story behind the Taj Mahal?**
It is the tomb Emperor Shah Jahan built for his wife Mumtaz Mahal, who died in 1631. Construction began in 1632 and the complex was finished by 1653.

**Was the Taj Mahal a temple?**
No evidence supports it. The Archaeological Survey of India told a court in 2017 that it is a tomb, and the Allahabad High Court dismissed a 2022 petition on the question.

**What is inside the 22 locked rooms?**
They are arched cells along a corridor in the platform, maintained by the ASI and closed for safety. Nothing of significance has been reported inside.

**Did Shah Jahan cut off the workers' hands?**
No. There is no historical record of it; it is a legend.

**Is the Black Taj Mahal real?**
No. The site across the river is the Mehtab Bagh garden.

**Who is buried in the Taj Mahal?**
Mumtaz Mahal and Shah Jahan, in the crypt beneath the cenotaphs in the main chamber.
